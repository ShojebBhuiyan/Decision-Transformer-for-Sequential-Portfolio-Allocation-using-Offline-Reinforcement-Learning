\begin{abstract}
We frame multi-asset portfolio allocation as an offline sequence-modeling problem using a Decision Transformer (DT). Historical trajectories synthesized from 18 behavior policies train a causal transformer to predict simplex portfolio weights conditioned on Return-to-Go (RTG). On Universe~A (14 assets, 2000--2025) we train DT, transformer behavior cloning (BC), and six RL baselines across five seeds, then backtest on 2020--2025. No learned method beats buy-and-hold on Sharpe after 10\,bp proportional costs. DT tracks BC almost exactly (Sharpe $0.97\pm0.12$ vs.\ $0.98\pm0.09$), and an RTG-quantile sweep is essentially flat, indicating unused return conditioning. Momentum attains the highest test CAGR (27.1\%) at the cost of turnover and drawdown; PPO collapses to equal weights.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Online reinforcement learning in financial markets is impractical due to transaction costs, non-stationarity, and the inability to replay history. Offline RL and sequence modeling offer an alternative: learn from fixed historical trajectories without environment interaction. The Decision Transformer~\cite{chen2021dt} reformulates RL as autoregressive modeling over $(\hat{R}_t, s_t, a_t)$ tuples, enabling conditioning on desired future returns at inference. We ask whether that conditioning improves sequential long-only allocation relative to classical policies and standard offline RL.

\section{Problem Formulation}
We define a Markov Decision Process over $N=14$ investable assets (including cash and a synthetic 10-year bond):
\begin{align}
    s_t &= X_t, \quad a_t \in \Delta^{N-1}, \\
    R_t &= \ln\!\left(a_t^\top y_t - \mu \sum_i |w_{i,t} - w'_{i,t-1}|\right),
\end{align}
where $y_t$ is the relative price-return vector, $\mu=0.001$ is the proportional transaction cost, and $\hat{R}_t = \sum_{t'=t}^{T} R_{t'}$ is the undiscounted Return-to-Go. Actions are long-only and sum to one (probability simplex).

\section{Data}
We use daily closing prices from the 30-year market dataset (1995--2025). After NYSE calendar alignment, limited forward-fill, and Universe~A construction from 2000-09-01 through 2025-12-30, we obtain 6{,}369 trading days. Feature states additionally flatten a 20-day lookback of per-asset returns, technical indicators, macro series, and event flags (state dimension 2{,}580). Non-investable series (VIX, WTI, term spreads) are features only. Train / validation / test splits are 2000--2016 / 2017--2019 / 2020--2025 (1{,}507 test days).

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Trajectory Synthesis}
Eighteen behavior policies generate 3{,}600 episodes of length $T=252$ on the training window: buy-and-hold, equal-weight, mean-variance (Ledoit--Wolf), min-variance, risk parity, momentum, Dirichlet-noisy variants of MVO/momentum/equal-weight ($\alpha\in\{5,15,50\}$), and random Dirichlet ($\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$). Rewards use the same log-return cost model as evaluation. RTG is stored undiscounted and standardized with training statistics ($\mu_{\mathrm{RTG}}=-0.002$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{RTG}}=0.139$).

\subsection{Decision Transformer}
A causal GPT with context $K=30$, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=192$, 4 layers, and 6 heads embeds interleaved (RTG, state, action) tokens. A softmax head enforces the simplex. Training minimizes MSE on the last-step action for 50 epochs, 200 steps per epoch, batch size 256, AdamW at $10^{-4}$, across seeds $\{42,123,456,789,1011\}$. At inference the RTG budget starts at the 90th percentile of training episode returns and decrements by the realized log reward; every 252 steps the budget is reset so multi-year rollouts stay in-distribution relative to training episodes.

\subsection{Baselines}
Classical: buy-and-hold, equal-weight, mean-variance, min-variance, risk parity, momentum. Imitation: transformer BC (same backbone, no RTG). Offline RL: TD3+BC, IQL, CQL. Online-style reference methods trained on the offline buffer (not true environment interaction): PPO, SAC, A2C.

\section{Experiments}
\textbf{Metrics:} CAGR, Sharpe (2\% annual risk-free rate), Sortino, Calmar, maximum drawdown, mean daily turnover, VaR/CVaR.

\textbf{Inference:} Each of 40 checkpoints is rolled out on the test window. Learned tables report the mean across five seeds. Walk-forward uses six 2-year folds; folds starting on or before 2016-12-31 are in-sample and are not read as performance.

\textbf{Statistics:} Circular block-bootstrap 95\% CIs (block size 20, 1{,}000 replicates) and Ledoit--Wolf tests compare each Sharpe to buy-and-hold. Deflated Sharpe~\cite{bailey2014dsr} uses $n_{\mathrm{trials}}=46$ (every classical strategy and every seed).

DT does not improve on BC: mean Sharpes $0.966$ vs.\ $0.976$, with overlapping seed ranges. Neither beats buy-and-hold. Block-bootstrap CIs for DT versus buy-and-hold exclude zero (DT worse) on two of five seeds; the other three CIs include zero. CQL has the highest mean CAGR ($34.3\%$) but Sharpe only $0.78$ because volatility ($34.6\%$) and max drawdown ($-50\%$) explode; seed 789 alone posts $53\%$ CAGR. A2C is unstable (turnover $>1$ per day). With $n_{\mathrm{trials}}=46$, every deflated Sharpe is $\approx 0$: after selection-bias correction, no strategy, including buy-and-hold, is distinguishable from luck.

\subsection{RTG calibration}
Sweeping target RTG over training-episode quantiles $\{0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9\}$ (budget reset every 252 days) yields nearly identical realized log returns (mean $\approx 1.17$ across quantiles). Mean Sharpe by quantile is $1.01$, $1.00$, $1.00$, $0.97$, $0.97$---slightly \emph{inverse}. Conditioning on a higher desired return does not produce a higher realized return, consistent with DT $\approx$ BC.

\section{Discussion}
On this close-only, long-only universe, a cost-aware equal-weight/buy-and-hold portfolio is a formidable baseline. Sequence models reproduce that allocation with extra turnover and do not extract a Sharpe premium from RTG. Offline RL either collapses (PPO), underperforms (TD3+BC, IQL), or inflates return by taking more risk (CQL). The ``online'' agents are single-step updates on an offline buffer and should not be read as a fair online comparison.

\textbf{Limitations.} Close-only data preclude volume or microstructure features. WTI's negative print (2020-04-20) is a feature only. The 252-day RTG reset is a methodological choice so that multi-year tests stay in-distribution; calibration is therefore per window, not against a single six-year target. Context-length and transaction-cost ablations, and Universe~B (Bitcoin + aluminum from 2014-09-17), were not part of this run.

\textbf{Future work.} Universe~B robustness, $K\in\{10,20,30,50\}$ and $\mu$ sweeps, and a less punitive trial count for deflated Sharpe (algorithm-level rather than per-seed).
